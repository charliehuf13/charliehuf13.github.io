Academic Trajectory

I am completing a B.S. in Biology at Oregon State University (Spring 2026) with a minor in Biochemistry & Molecular Biology, and I will begin an M.S. in Bioinformatics at Georgia Tech in Fall 2026. This path reflects my long-term focus on research at the intersection of genomics, microbial biology, and computation.

Research Profile

I am a researcher who integrates computational and experimental methods to study biological variation. My projects center on comparative genomics, genome quality assessment, and microbial system characterization, with emphasis on turning large datasets into biologically meaningful conclusions.

Research Experience

Chang Lab (Bioinformatics / Genomics / HPC)

I develop and implement comparative genomics workflows across 100+ genomes using GENESPACE, MUMmer, and nf-core pipelines. This includes assembly and annotation quality assessment with BUSCO, along with SLURM-based HPC workflow design and Singularity-based reproducible execution.

Halsey Lab (Microbiology / Molecular Biology)

I designed and executed a PCR-based 16S rRNA identification workflow spanning DNA extraction, PCR amplification, gel electrophoresis, and sequencing. This work supports microbial consortium research in biofuel-related systems, including identification of associated contaminants and interpretation of mixed-culture biology.

Technical Skills

Bioinformatics: GENESPACE, MUMmer, nf-core, BUSCO, Helixer.

HPC/Tools: SLURM, Linux, Singularity, Nextflow, Git.

Molecular Biology: PCR, DNA extraction, gel electrophoresis, 16S rRNA organism identification workflows, microbial culturing, and experimental design.

Research Interests

My research interests include genomic variation across isolates and species, comparative genomics at scale, and microbial systems where experimental and computational analyses can directly inform one another. I am also building machine learning skills to support future bioinformatics research on complex biological datasets.
